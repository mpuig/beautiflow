Hermes’ Delivery Office

Terracotta & ink

Hermes serves two customer accounts. Standard deliveries use a shared worker cell across two AZs; an enterprise customer gets a separate account, queue, worker and ledger.

Authoring prompt

The design brief for this example.

Design a fictional cross-account messaging SaaS. Bind authenticated publisher accounts to tenants; never trust a tenant ID in event detail. Show operator authentication, tenant-route administration, a pooled SQS worker cell with Fargate consumers in two private AZ placements, and a dedicated delivery account with its own bus, queue, Lambda, ledger and DLQ. Keep SQS and DynamoDB regional, outside AZ boxes.

Architecture assumptions & omissions
  • Tenant A and B are illustrative customers. Workload roles sign PutEvents; resource policies and an account-to-tenant allowlist constrain ingress and routing. Multi-tenant publishers within a single customer account would need a separately validated ingress contract.
  • AZ A/B contain private Fargate tasks, not independent SQS or DynamoDB instances. Assumed SQS interface endpoints have ENIs in enabled AZs; DynamoDB uses a gateway endpoint. Endpoint and security-group configuration is not drawn.
  • Cross-account bus forwarding requires a sender role, destination bus policy and explicit target permissions. Dedicated workers have no intended pooled-state access. These policies are design requirements, not validated IAM documents.
  • Queue arrows denote polling and configured redrive. Consumers need idempotency, tenant-scoped state access and a privileged, audited replay process. A shared queue is a noisy-neighbour trade-off; stricter service tiers may need more queue partitions. Recipient transport and autoscaling policies are omitted.
